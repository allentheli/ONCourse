About

Why I built ONCourse

I'm a practicing medical oncologist. Most days I sit across from someone who has just learned they have cancer and walk them through a plan that will take the better part of a year.

Treatment has become remarkably effective, and remarkably complicated. We rarely treat a cancer with one method anymore. Chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted drugs, surgery, radiation, and hormone therapy are combined and sequenced, and the sequence itself often depends on what surgery or a scan reveals along the way.

In the room, it makes sense. By the time a patient is home, the order is hard to hold onto, and "what happens next?" is the question I hear most. I wanted something I could hand a patient that showed the whole road: where they are, what comes next, where the path might fork, and why this plan was chosen for them. So I built ONCourse.

Allen Li, MD
Medical oncologist, Washington State

What we believe

There is no one-size-fits-all treatment. Two people with the same diagnosis can rightly end up on different plans. The best decision weighs the evidence, the specific cancer, the person's health, and what they want from their life. That decision should be shared, and a shared decision needs a shared picture.

Evidence first, plainly stated. Every pathway in ONCourse traces to a clinical trial or guideline, with sources a physician can click. The plain-language text says what a treatment is and how it is given, without promises the evidence does not support.

Families are part of the care team. A printed map on the refrigerator, or a link on a daughter's phone, means everyone who is helping can help in the same direction.

The oncologist owns the plan. ONCourse never decides a treatment. It starts from evidence-reviewed pathways and lets the treating physician adjust, annotate, and take responsibility for what the patient receives. ONCourse makes the plan visible.

How ONCourse treats evidence

Pathways are checked against the primary publication and a structured protocol reference before release, and each records the date it was last reviewed and who reviewed it. Between-phase intervals follow the trial protocol where one is specified; where practice varies, the default is a typical value the physician is expected to adjust. New approvals and label changes are reviewed as they arrive; see the update log and the sources for every pathway.

Privacy

ONCourse has no accounts, no server, and no analytics, and it serves its own fonts, so using it makes no requests to third parties. A share link or QR code contains the plan itself, encoded in the link, and nothing else, which is why the page asks physicians never to type a patient's name. Physicians' saved pathways stay in their own browser unless they export them.
