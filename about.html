About

Treatment decisions belong to the people living with them.

A cancer diagnosis arrives with a plan that can span a year or more: chemotherapy, then surgery, then a wait for a pathology report that decides what happens next. Most of that plan lives in a physician's head and an infusion calendar. Patients and families are asked to trust a course they cannot see.

ONCourse exists to make the course visible. It gives physicians a fast way to draw the whole plan, the way they would sketch it on paper for a patient, and it gives patients something they can hold: what is happening, in what order, for how long, and why this plan was chosen for them.

What we believe

There is no one-size-fits-all treatment. Two people with the same diagnosis can rightly end up on different plans. The best decision weighs the evidence, the specific cancer, the person's health, and what they want from their life. That decision should be shared, and a shared decision needs a shared picture.

Evidence first, plainly stated. Every plan in ONCourse traces to a clinical trial or guideline, with sources a physician can click. The plain-language text says what the treatment is and how it is given, without promises the evidence does not support.

Families are part of the care team. A printed plan on the refrigerator, or a link on a daughter's phone, means everyone who is helping can help in the same direction.

The physician is the author. ONCourse never decides a treatment. It starts from verified regimens and lets the treating physician adjust, annotate, and take responsibility for what the patient receives.

How ONCourse treats evidence

Regimens are checked against the primary publication and a structured protocol reference before release, and each carries the date it was last reviewed. Between-phase intervals follow the trial protocol where one is specified; where practice varies, the default is a typical value the physician is expected to adjust. New approvals and label changes are reviewed as they arrive; see the update log.

Privacy

ONCourse has no accounts and no server. It runs entirely in the browser. A share link or QR code contains the plan itself, encoded in the link, and nothing else, which is why the page asks physicians never to type a patient's name. Physicians' saved regimens stay in their own browser unless they export them.
